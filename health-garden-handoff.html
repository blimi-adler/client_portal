What we built together

February to October 2026

Joel, we started working together in February 2026 with one clear goal from you: a structured, organized business where every person owns their part and the systems support the work instead of slowing it down.

At the start, the business had grown faster than its structure. Roles overlapped across four companies, most processes lived in people’s heads, and the software didn’t fit how Health Garden actually runs. Over the following months we worked through product, process, and people, one company and one team at a time.

How we got there

February: foundation and clarity

  • Held discovery meetings with every key team member across Health Garden, Honix, Sweet Valley, and Paterson.
  • Built a consolidated issues and observations list.
  • Mapped the team structure across all four companies and defined ownership per function.
  • Outlined the core processes for production, inventory, and sales.
  • Completed a software needs assessment and a phased software plan.
  • Aligned with you and Moshe on growth priorities and goals.

March: from plan to practice

  • Communicated and documented role adjustments across all companies.
  • Started a recurring meeting rhythm for leadership and key staff.
  • Documented core workflows in Notion as the single source of truth.
  • Completed the software map so each department knows its tools, and set up the priority systems.
  • Completed a financial review and documented the key findings.
  • Put a leadership accountability structure in place.

April: systems and people

  • Set up Asana in full: projects and teams, request forms, 1:1 agendas for every employee, and a leadership dashboard and issues tracker. Trained every employee.
  • Hired and onboarded a new Operations Director with an offer letter and a 30-60-90 day plan.
  • Built a sales metrics and weekly reporting structure, refocused on existing and recurring customers.
  • Simplified the Honix workflow with a NetSuite and Asana plan.
  • Set the financial rhythm: monthly P&L by the 5th, weekly cash flow review with you, and a mapped AP approval flow. Built an overhead expense template by department.
  • Kicked off NetSuite (ERP on April 24, CRM on April 28) with weekly check-ins.

May to July: building toward go-live

  • Ran a Health Garden team leadership session and set a weekly team meeting.
  • Set scorecard metrics: gross profit %, net profit %, online orders by platform, and open orders.
  • Prepared NetSuite go-live: data cutoff plan, chart of accounts, and a custom record for new product sourcing.
  • Set six-month objectives with you in July, with target dates for NetSuite, team structure, operations, and finance.

August: go-live and Sweet Valley

  • NetSuite went live as the primary platform for Health Garden and Honix.
  • Facilitated a Six Questions session with Sweet Valley to set focus, priorities, and issues.
  • Mapped every Sweet Valley process and SOP, with issues and improvements identified.
  • Started the weekly Sweet Valley L10 meeting.
  • Built a weekly schedule for your role.
  • Walked through Paterson with the NetSuite partner.
  • Met with the sales team to re-clarify roles, processes, and objectives.

September: SOPs, sales, and financials

  • Documented Health Garden SOPs in sessions with you, Ruddi, Gisella, and Phil. Reviewed the FoodReady checklists.
  • Mapped all Honix SOPs with Joseph.
  • Confirmed Theresa as head of sales with Naftoli and Joseph reporting to her. Set biweekly open and closed order metrics, weekly target items, monthly specials, and a unified pricing sheet.
  • Reviewed financial projections and a plan per company with you, Moshe, and Dina. Built cash flow reports for all companies.
  • Onboarded a new NetSuite service provider and mapped the configuration plan across all three companies.
  • Trained the team on the NetSuite CRM for day-to-day use.
  • Created daily schedules for the floor manager and account management roles.

October: finishing the playbook

  • Finalized the Sweet Valley production SOPs.
  • Documented the Paterson warehouse SOPs.
  • Defined 24 roles with purpose and responsibilities, plus full job descriptions for the Operations Director and Floor Manager.

What to keep going

  • Hold every role accountable with the SOPs and weekly meetings already in place.
  • Study the numbers monthly and keep sales focused on clear targets.
  • Close the NetSuite inventory gap and organize the warehouse.
  • Keep refining workflows, and bring in AI where it saves time.
  • Build a marketing strategy when capacity allows.

    Production / Floor Manager

    Sweet Valley

    Oversees daily production at Sweet Valley so orders move from intake to fulfillment on time, to quality standards, with a team that’s accountable. A hands-on leadership role for the full production floor.

    Orders and production
    Verify incoming orders are complete · Coordinate private label inventory and labeling, including Amazon requirements · Run the full production cycle: materials, prep, blending, staging, assembly line, close-out · Release paperwork at each handoff · Make sure POs close and invoices go out on time · Track time per order
    Team
    Run the daily floor schedule and assignments · Monitor efficiency and reduce waste in labor and materials · Hold the floor team to SOPs and standards
    Supplies
    Purchase and track supplies · Keep backup inventory · Catch shortages before they stop production
    Equipment
    Machine running schedule · Repairs and downtime · Cleaning and facility standards
    Quality and safety
    Pre-shift checks on dress code, pests, and chemicals · FoodReady checklist · Pre-op inspection · Metal detector form every 2 hours · Contractor log · Organic cleaning process
